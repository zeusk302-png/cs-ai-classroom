Subtopic 1.1

Row and column interpretations of equations

If we know the prices of two bundles, can we determine the individual prices of a pencil and a notebook?

Preparation and learning goals

Preparation

  • Linear expressions: 2x+y adds two copies of x and one copy of y.
  • Linear equations: adding or subtracting the same quantity on both sides preserves solutions.
  • Ordered pairs: reversing the variable order in (x,y) generally gives a different candidate solution.

Learning goals

  • Define a solution set by simultaneous satisfaction of every equation.
  • Express the same linear system using row, column, and matrix viewpoints.
  • Substitute solutions into the original equations and check whether the model permits them.

Satisfy several equations at the same time

A linear equation in real unknowns x₁,…,xₙ is a₁x₁+⋯+aₙxₙ=b. The coefficients and b are given constants. Products between variables and powers such as a variable squared are absent. A solution of a system is an ordered list of n numbers satisfying every equation simultaneously.

The equation x+y=5 has all solutions (t,5−t), t∈ℝ, rather than just one. Requiring 2x+y=7 as well leaves only common solutions. The pair (1,4) satisfies x+y=5 but gives 6≠7 in the second equation, so it is not a solution of the system.

Row viewpoint: intersect the conditions

For a two-variable equation whose coefficients are not both zero, the solutions form a line in the coordinate plane. Each row is one condition; the solution set is the intersection of the lines. Two intersecting lines give one point, distinct parallel lines give no common point, and coincident lines give infinitely many points.

One nontrivial equation in three variables describes a plane. In higher dimensions, use the same simultaneous-condition reasoning even without a picture. The equation 0x+0y=0 describes the whole plane, while 0x+0y=1 describes the empty set. Not every equation is automatically a line.

Column viewpoint: build the target vector

Stacking x+y=5 and 2x+y=7 gives x(1,2)+y(1,1)=(5,7). Each column collects one variable’s effect across every equation. The values x,y specify how much of each column to add to reach the target.

Write A=[[1,1],[2,1]], u=(x,y), b=(5,7), giving Au=b. Row i produces aᵢ₁x+aᵢ₂y. Multiplying each column of A by its variable value and adding gives the same result. The augmented matrix [A|b] records coefficients and constants; its last column is not a new unknown.

A worked example, step by step

A pencil price x and notebook price y, in thousand Korean won, satisfy x+y=5 and 2x+y=7. Find the solution and check it using rows and columns. Assume identical unit prices in both bundles, no bundle discounts, and no extra charges.

  1. Subtract the first equation from the second to obtain x=2.
  2. Substitution into the first gives 2+y=5, so y=3.
  3. Row check: 2+3=5 and 2×2+3=7.
  4. Column check: 2(1,2)+3(1,1)=(2,4)+(3,3)=(5,7).
  5. The price constraints x,y≥0 are also satisfied.

(x,y)=(2,3): the pencil costs 2000 won and the notebook 3000 won. Both viewpoints express the same linear system.

Try it with paper and the lesson

On paper, write x+2y=8, 3x+y=9 as a column-vector equation. Substitute (2,3) to check it.

Show hint

Collect the x coefficients 1,3 into one column and the y coefficients 2,1 into another.

Show solution

x(1,3)+y(2,1)=(8,9). Since 2(1,3)+3(2,1)=(2,6)+(6,3)=(8,9), (2,3) is a solution. The original equations also give 2+6=8 and 6+3=9.

Misconception and counterexample

Two equations in two unknowns always have a unique solution.

x+y=5 and 2x+2y=10 state the same condition, giving infinitely many solutions. Changing the second constant to 11 gives no solution. Equation and variable counts alone do not establish independence or consistency.

Check your understanding

Does satisfying just one equation make a solution of the system?

Show answer and reasoning

Every equation must be satisfied simultaneously.

The solution set is the intersection of the individual solution sets.

Solve x+y=4, x−y=2.

Show answer and reasoning

(3,1).

Adding gives 2x=6, then y=1. The values 3±1 are 4 and 2.

Solve x+y=1, x+y=2.

Show answer and reasoning

There is no solution.

The same sum cannot simultaneously equal two different numbers.

Explore further

Go further: Au=b has a solution exactly when b is a linear combination of A’s columns. For A=[[1,2],[2,4]], every column combination has the form (t,2t). Calculate why b=(3,6) is reachable but (3,7) is not.

Subtopic 1.2

Row echelon form and solution sets

Can we simplify the equations while keeping every original solution?

Preparation and learning goals

Preparation

  • Fraction arithmetic: 2y=6 gives y=3.
  • System solutions: satisfy every row simultaneously.
  • Row notation Rᵢ: the whole ith row of the table.

Learning goals

  • Explain preservation of the solution set by reversing each of the three elementary row operations.
  • Identify contradictions in echelon form and perform back substitution.
  • Distinguish a zero row from a contradictory row.

Three changes that preserve solutions

Elementary operations on an augmented matrix are exchanging two rows, multiplying a row by a nonzero constant, and adding a constant multiple of a different row to a row. Apply each operation to the entire row, including the constant term. Reverse them by the same exchange, reciprocal scaling, or adding the opposite multiple.

A solution of the original equations satisfies the new ones. Conversely, reversing the operations shows that a solution of the new equations satisfies the original ones. This two-way reasoning establishes preservation of the solution set. Multiplying a row by zero has no inverse and loses information, so it is not allowed.

The structure of echelon form

In row echelon form, all zero rows are at the bottom, the first nonzero entry of each nonzero row lies farther right than the one above it, and all entries below each such entry are zero. That first entry is a leading entry and its position is a pivot position. A leading entry need not be 1.

Work from the left: when a nonzero entry is available, move its row upward by exchanging rows and eliminate entries below it in the same column. Continue to the next row and columns to its right. If all coefficient entries are zero but the constant is c≠0, the row says 0=c, a contradiction. The row [0,0|0] adds no condition.

Read solutions from bottom to top

Echelon form lets you solve later variables first. For example, [1,2|8],[0,−5|−15] gives y=3 from −5y=−15, then x=8−2×3=2 from the upper row. This is back substitution.

After elimination, substitute into the original equations to catch arithmetic errors. Different operation orders and multipliers can produce different echelon forms, but the preserved solution set must agree. A zero row at the bottom does not by itself imply that there are no solutions.

A worked example, step by step

Transform x+2y=8, 3x+y=9 into echelon form and determine the solution set.

  1. [A|b]=[[1,2|8],[3,1|9]].
  2. R₂←R₂−3R₁ gives [[1,2|8],[0,−5|−15]]. Change the constant too: 9−3×8=−15.
  3. From −5y=−15, y=3. From x+6=8, x=2.
  4. Original-equation check: 2+2×3=8 and 3×2+3=9.

The solution set is {(2,3)}. There is no contradictory row and both variables are determined.

Try it with paper and the lesson

Apply R₂←R₂−2R₁ to x+y=5 and 2x+2y=c. Compare c=10 with c=11.

Show hint

All lower-row coefficients become zero; its constant becomes c−10.

Show solution

For c=10, [0,0|0] leaves only x+y=5, with infinitely many solutions (t,5−t), t∈ℝ. For c=11, [0,0|1] says 0=1, so the solution set is empty.

Misconception and counterexample

Overwriting one row with another always preserves solutions.

R₂←R₁ loses the original information in R₂. Replacing x=1,y=2 by x=1,x=1 makes y unrestricted. The permitted operation R₂←R₂+cR₁ retains the old R₂ and can be reversed.

Check your understanding

May you multiply a row by zero?

Show answer and reasoning

No.

Only nonzero scaling has an inverse that restores the information.

What condition does [0,2|6] express?

Show answer and reasoning

y=3.

It means 0x+2y=6.

Does [0,0|0] mean there is no solution?

Show answer and reasoning

That row itself is not contradictory.

0=0 is true for all variable values; inspect the other rows.

Explore further

Go further: an elementary row operation can be represented by multiplying on the left by an invertible matrix E. Ax=b and EAx=Eb have the same solution set because E⁻¹ can be applied to both sides. Constructing E connects to the next unit on matrix operations.

Subtopic 1.3

Pivots and free variables

Can two equations always determine a single answer for three variables?

Preparation and learning goals

Preparation

  • Echelon form: the leading entry in a lower row lies farther to the right.
  • Parameter: t is a freely chosen real number.
  • Vector notation: (x,y,z) groups three values in the stated order.

Learning goals

  • Distinguish pivot variables in coefficient columns from nonpivot free variables.
  • Parametrize every solution of a consistent linear system.
  • State the conditions under which pivot counts determine unique or infinitely many solutions.

What is determined, and what remains free?

In echelon form obtained by eliminating the coefficient matrix, variables corresponding to pivot columns are pivot variables; those in nonpivot variable columns are free variables. The augmented matrix’s constant column is not a variable column. A pivot there indicates a contradictory row.

In x+2z=5, y−z=1, the x,y columns are pivot columns and z is free. Choosing z=t determines x=5−2t and y=1+t. “Free” means t can be chosen first, not that the other variables are independent of t.

Decide the number of solutions in the right order

First check for a contradictory row. If one exists, there is no solution regardless of the number of free columns. Otherwise, choose arbitrary real values for the free variables and back substitute. No free variables gives a unique solution; at least one gives infinitely many solutions over the reals.

If the coefficient matrix has r pivots and n variable columns, there are n−r free variables. This counts parameters of the solution set only when a solution exists. Extra equations do not increase r when they are redundant.

Every solution, rather than one solution

Writing (x,y,z)=(5,1,0)+t(−2,1,1) shows one particular solution at t=0 and a direction of motion. Substitution into the original equations cancels the t terms, verifying a solution for every t.

Conversely, any solution has some z=t, and the two equations force x=5−2t,y=1+t. This proves that no solution lies outside the expression. Finding a few solutions differs from describing the whole solution set. A homogeneous system Ax=0 always has the zero-vector solution, so consistency is automatic.

A worked example, step by step

Find all real solutions of x+y+z=6, 2x+y+3z=10.

  1. R₂←R₂−2R₁ changes the second equation to −y+z=−2, so y=2+z.
  2. The first gives x=6−(2+z)−z=4−2z. The pivot columns are x,y; z is free.
  3. Set z=t: (x,y,z)=(4−2t,2+t,t)=(4,2,0)+t(−2,1,1).
  4. First equation: 4−2t+2+t+t=6. Second: 8−4t+2+t+3t=10.
  5. Every solution of the original system has some z=t, forcing the same expressions, so this describes all solutions.

{(4−2t,2+t,t):t∈ℝ}. Giving only (4,2,0) at t=0 misses the rest of the solution set.

Try it with paper and the lesson

For x−y+2z=3, express every solution using y=s,z=t. Check s=1,t=2.

Show hint

Only the x column has a pivot. Choose the other two variables freely.

Show solution

x=3+s−2t, so (x,y,z)=(3+s−2t,s,t), s,t∈ℝ. For s=1,t=2, the result is (0,1,2), and 0−1+4=3. Two independent parameters remain.

Misconception and counterexample

Free variables always imply infinitely many solutions.

Consistency is required first. In x+z=1, 0x+0y+0z=2, y,z are nonpivot columns, but 0=2 makes the system inconsistent. This unit’s infinite-solution conclusion applies over infinite number systems such as the reals or complex numbers.

Check your understanding

With five variables and three coefficient-matrix pivots, how many free variables are there?

Show answer and reasoning

2.

5−3=2. Parametrizing actual solutions also requires no contradiction.

For x+z=2,y−z=0, what solution has z=3?

Show answer and reasoning

(−1,3,3).

x=2−3=−1 and y=z=3.

Can a homogeneous system have no solution?

Show answer and reasoning

No.

Setting every variable to zero makes every row 0=0.

Explore further

Go further: if uₚ is a particular solution of Ax=b, then every solution is uₚ+v with Av=0. Show one direction using A(u−uₚ)=b−b=0 and the other using A(uₚ+v)=b. Free variables describe directions in this homogeneous solution space.

Subtopic 1.4

Gauss–Jordan elimination

Can we read all variable relationships from an echelon form without further substitution?

Preparation and learning goals

Preparation

  • Elementary row operations: apply them to the entire row, including the constant.
  • Pivots and free variables: distinguish variable columns from the final constant column.
  • Fraction arithmetic: divide a row by its pivot value to make the leading entry 1.

Learning goals

  • Check the conditions of reduced row echelon form.
  • Record the Gauss–Jordan process that eliminates above and below each pivot.
  • Read every solution from the result and check it in the original equations.

One step beyond echelon form

Reduced row echelon form (RREF) satisfies the echelon conditions and additionally has leading entry 1 in every nonzero row, with all other entries in that leading-entry column zero. Entries in free-variable columns do not all have to be zero.

Gaussian elimination clears below pivots to produce echelon form, then uses back substitution. Gauss–Jordan makes pivots 1 and clears above them as well, giving reduced form. Both use elementary row operations preserving the solution set and solve the same linear system.

The procedure and a zero candidate pivot

From the left, find a column containing an available nonzero entry and exchange rows to put it in the current row. Divide that row by the pivot, then eliminate every other entry in the same column by subtracting multiples. Move down one row and continue to the right. If the current column is zero in all remaining rows, skip it.

You cannot divide by zero. If the first entry is zero but a lower nonzero entry is available, a row exchange lets you continue. This does not mean the whole system has no solution. Use exact fractions by hand; small pivots and rounding in computer floating-point arithmetic are treated separately in numerical analysis.

Reading the result and uniqueness

The reduced form [1,0,2|5],[0,1,−1|1] means x+2z=5,y−z=1. With z=t, read the solution as (5−2t,1+t,t). Unit vectors appearing in pivot columns do not make the entire coefficient matrix an identity matrix.

A given matrix has a unique RREF in exact arithmetic, although the sequence of operations need not be unique. A general uniqueness proof comes later through row-space theory. In this unit, perform different elimination orders by hand and check that their results agree. Verify final solutions in the original equations.

A worked example, step by step

Solve 2x+y=7, x−y=2 using Gauss–Jordan elimination.

  1. Start with [[2,1|7],[1,−1|2]]. Exchange R₁↔R₂ to obtain [[1,−1|2],[2,1|7]].
  2. R₂←R₂−2R₁ gives [[1,−1|2],[0,3|3]].
  3. R₂←R₂/3 gives [[1,−1|2],[0,1|1]].
  4. R₁←R₁+R₂ gives [[1,0|3],[0,1|1]].
  5. Thus x=3,y=1. The original equations give 2×3+1=7 and 3−1=2.

RREF [[1,0|3],[0,1|1]], with solution set {(3,1)}.

Try it with paper and the lesson

Eliminate 0x+y=2, x+y=5. Record how you handle the initially zero candidate pivot.

Show hint

Exchange the first and second rows, then work through the x and y columns.

Show solution

Exchange rows in [[0,1|2],[1,1|5]] to obtain [[1,1|5],[0,1|2]]. Then R₁←R₁−R₂ gives [[1,0|3],[0,1|2]]. Thus x=3,y=2, and the original equations give 2=2 and 3+2=5.

Misconception and counterexample

Gauss–Jordan always makes the left side an identity matrix.

x+y=1, 2x+2y=2 gives RREF [[1,1|1],[0,0|0]]. Since y is free, the coefficient part cannot be the identity matrix. Every variable is determined only when the system is consistent and each variable column has a pivot.

Check your understanding

In reduced form, what must appear above a pivot 1?

Show answer and reasoning

0.

That 1 must be the only nonzero entry in its pivot column.

Is [[1,0|4],[0,2|6]] already in reduced form?

Show answer and reasoning

No. Divide the second row by 2.

Every leading entry must be 1. The solution is (4,3).

Must elimination stop when the first entry is zero?

Show answer and reasoning

No. Find a lower nonzero entry and exchange rows, or skip the column if none exists.

One unavailable candidate pivot does not imply that the entire system is impossible.

Explore further

Go further: for [[2,1|7],[1,−1|2]], start by dividing R₁ by 2 without exchanging rows. R₂←R₂−R₁ gives [0,−3/2|−3/2]. Divide R₂ by −3/2, then subtract R₂/2 from R₁ to reach the same RREF.
